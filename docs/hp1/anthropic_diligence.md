# Anthropic pre-IPO / IPO diligence as of June 12, 2026

## Summary verdict

Anthropic has **confidentially submitted a draft Form S-1**, but there is **no public S-1/prospectus with IPO economics** as of this diligence cut: Anthropic said on June 1, 2026 that the share count and price were not set, and the known EDGAR record checked for Anthropic-related CIK 0001979832 shows only a 2023 Form D for an Anthropic SPV rather than a public S-1 filing ([Anthropic, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec); [SEC submissions for CIK 0001979832, accessed Jun. 12, 2026](https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0001979832.json)).

The best verified revenue anchor is Anthropic’s own May 28, 2026 statement that run-rate revenue crossed **$47B earlier in May**, which makes the May 2026 Series H valuation of **$965B post-money** equal to **20.5x run-rate revenue** ([Anthropic Series H, May 28, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h)).

The investor should **not underwrite a $1T entry price without a public S-1** because revenue quality, gross margin, cash-flow durability, customer concentration, related-party economics, governance rights, and lock-up supply are not yet disclosed in prospectus form ([Anthropic, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec); [TechCrunch, Jun. 1, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/anthropic-files-to-go-public/)).

Recommended ceiling: **14x-16x verified run-rate revenue**, or **$658B-$752B** on the verified $47B run-rate, with a hard preference to wait for public S-1 financial statements before deploying a $300K-$500K position ([Anthropic Series H, May 28, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h)).

A stretch valuation above **18x-20x** should require disclosed public-company-quality evidence of durable 70%+ gross margin, low customer concentration, manageable net cash burn after compute commitments, and clean IPO governance/lock-up terms; otherwise the current **20.5x** Series H mark already prices Anthropic richer than NVIDIA’s **12.6x FY1 revenue** multiple and CoreWeave’s **6.7x FY1 revenue** multiple while lacking comparable public disclosure ([Yahoo Finance NVDA analysis/key statistics, accessed Jun. 12, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/NVDA/analysis/); [Yahoo Finance CRWV analysis/key statistics, accessed Jun. 12, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CRWV/analysis/); [Anthropic Series H, May 28, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h)).

## 1. IPO status

### Confirmed status

Anthropic announced on June 1, 2026 that **Anthropic, PBC confidentially submitted a draft registration statement on Form S-1 to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission** for a proposed IPO of common stock ([Anthropic, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec)).

Anthropic’s announcement says the submission “gives us the option to go public after the SEC completes its review,” and it states that the IPO depends on market conditions and other factors ([Anthropic, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec)).

Anthropic’s announcement also says the number of shares to be offered and the price have **not** been set ([Anthropic, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec)).

NPR reported on June 1, 2026 that Anthropic had “confidentially submitted” a Form S-1 draft and that the number of shares and price had not yet been set ([NPR, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/01/nx-s1-5843199/anthropic-ipo-filing-ai-large)).

TechCrunch reported on June 1, 2026 that Anthropic had filed confidentially for an IPO, had not listed the number of shares, and had not set the price ([TechCrunch, Jun. 1, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/anthropic-files-to-go-public/)).

The Guardian reported on June 1, 2026 that Anthropic filed confidentially, did not disclose the valuation it would target, did not make public other terms, did not give an exact timeline, and did not reveal the number of shares to be offered ([The Guardian, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/jun/01/anthropic-ai-ipo)).

### What is **not** confirmed

There is no public prospectus in the evidence reviewed, and Anthropic’s own Rule 135 announcement expressly withholds share count and pricing terms ([Anthropic, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec)).

No confirmed pricing range, exchange, ticker, underwriting syndicate, share-class recapitalization, lock-up schedule, directed-share program, or selling-stockholder component is available from the public materials reviewed ([Anthropic, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec); [TechCrunch, Jun. 1, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/anthropic-files-to-go-public/)).

The phrase “has filed for IPO” is directionally true only if it means **confidential draft S-1 submission**, and it is misleading if it implies a **public S-1 with IPO terms** ([Anthropic, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec)).

The known EDGAR submission check for CIK 0001979832 shows a single Form D filed June 14, 2023 for “IBD VENTURES, LLC - ANTHROPIC SERIES O,” not a public Anthropic operating-company S-1 ([SEC submissions for CIK 0001979832, accessed Jun. 12, 2026](https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0001979832.json)).

### Press speculation versus confirmed terms

The nearly $1T valuation chatter is grounded in Anthropic’s May 28, 2026 private Series H announcement at **$965B post-money**, not in a disclosed IPO price range ([Anthropic Series H, May 28, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h)).

NPR described Anthropic as valued at almost $1T because the June 1 IPO filing followed the May 28 financing that valued the company at $965B post-money ([NPR, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/01/nx-s1-5843199/anthropic-ipo-filing-ai-large)).

The Guardian wrote that Anthropic did not disclose the valuation it would target on the stock market, so any IPO valuation number remains speculation until a public filing or pricing announcement appears ([The Guardian, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/jun/01/anthropic-ai-ipo)).

## 4. Ceiling recommendation

### Recommended maximum entry valuation

The recommended ceiling is **14x-16x verified run-rate revenue**, which equals **$658B-$752B** on Anthropic’s verified $47B run-rate from May 2026 ([Anthropic Series H, May 28, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h)).

A conservative bid should cluster near **14x** if the investor cannot review the public S-1 before purchase, because no audited public disclosure yet verifies Anthropic’s gross margin, net burn, customer concentration, revenue-recognition policy, compute obligations, related-party economics, voting control, or lock-up schedule ([Anthropic, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec)).

A stretch bid near **16x** can be justified only by the extraordinary verified acceleration from about $9B run-rate at the end of 2025 to more than $47B in May 2026, plus the evidence that enterprise adoption is broadening across high-spend customers ([Anthropic/Amazon, Apr. 20, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute); [Anthropic Series H, May 28, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h)).

The current **$965B** Series H mark equals **20.5x** the verified $47B run-rate, so buying at roughly the latest private valuation requires either accepting an OpenAI-like scarcity premium or assuming the S-1 will validate margins and concentration better than currently observable public evidence ([Anthropic Series H, May 28, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h); [OpenAI, Mar. 31, 2026](https://openai.com/index/accelerating-the-next-phase-ai/)).

### Multiple framework

The base ceiling discounts Anthropic from OpenAI’s **35.5x annualized revenue** mark because Anthropic lacks public S-1 disclosure and because OpenAI’s funding announcement disclosed $2B monthly revenue but did not provide audited public financial statements either ([OpenAI, Mar. 31, 2026](https://openai.com/index/accelerating-the-next-phase-ai/); [Anthropic, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec)).

The base ceiling applies a premium to NVIDIA’s **12.6x FY1 revenue** multiple because Anthropic’s verified run-rate growth from year-end 2025 to May 2026 is far faster than NVIDIA’s FY1 consensus revenue growth of **81.4%**, but the premium is capped because Anthropic’s AI-lab economics are more opaque and compute commitments are structurally heavier ([Anthropic/Amazon, Apr. 20, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute); [Anthropic Series H, May 28, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h); [Yahoo Finance NVDA analysis/key statistics, accessed Jun. 12, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/NVDA/analysis/)).

The base ceiling stays far below Palantir’s **39.7x FY1 revenue** multiple because Palantir is a public software company with **84.1% gross margin** in Yahoo Finance data, while Anthropic’s gross margin is not yet public-filed and may be pressured by inference and training costs ([Yahoo Finance PLTR analysis/key statistics, accessed Jun. 12, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/PLTR/analysis/); [Yahoo Finance/The Information summary, Nov. 4, 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/anthropic-projects-70b-revenue-2028-164854310.html)).

The downside valuation reference is CoreWeave’s **6.7x FY1 revenue** public multiple because both companies depend on scarce AI compute, but Anthropic deserves a premium if its S-1 confirms that Claude software revenue carries much higher gross margin and lower customer concentration than AI infrastructure leasing ([Yahoo Finance CRWV analysis/key statistics, accessed Jun. 12, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CRWV/analysis/); [CoreWeave SEC prospectus, Mar. 27, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000119312525067651/d899798d424b4.htm)).

### What would change the ceiling

The ceiling should move up if a public S-1 shows 70%+ gross margin, positive free cash flow after compute payments, diversified enterprise/API customers, standard public-company governance, modest related-party balances, and lock-up terms that reduce day-1 float-squeeze risk ([Anthropic, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec)).

The ceiling should move down if growth decelerates below the current run-rate trajectory, if Q2 profit excludes major stock-based compensation or capitalized training expense, if a few API or cloud customers dominate revenue, if hyperscaler investment is economically circular, or if public investors receive low-vote shares under a governance structure that limits accountability ([CNBC, May 20, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/20/anthropic-revenue-explosive-growth-ipo-profitable-quarter.html); [Harvard Law Review, Apr. 10, 2025](https://harvardlawreview.org/print/vol-138/amoral-drift-in-ai-corporate-governance/); [Anthropic/Amazon, Apr. 20, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute)).

## 5. Red flags and gating diligence items

### Governance and control

Anthropic is a Delaware public benefit corporation with a public benefit purpose described by Harvard Law Review as “the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity” ([Harvard Law Review, Apr. 10, 2025](https://harvardlawreview.org/print/vol-138/amoral-drift-in-ai-corporate-governance/)).

Harvard Law Review states that Anthropic combines the PBC form with a “Long-Term Benefit Trust,” and that “Class T” shareholders alone will elect three of five board directors after a trigger tied to May 24, 2027 or eight months after obtaining $6B of investments, whichever comes first ([Harvard Law Review, Apr. 10, 2025](https://harvardlawreview.org/print/vol-138/amoral-drift-in-ai-corporate-governance/)).

This structure may reduce ordinary shareholder influence and may be mission-protective, but it also means a public investor must inspect the S-1 charter, voting-rights, director-election, conversion, and fiduciary-risk disclosures before buying ([Harvard Law Review, Apr. 10, 2025](https://harvardlawreview.org/print/vol-138/amoral-drift-in-ai-corporate-governance/); [TechCrunch, Jun. 1, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/anthropic-files-to-go-public/)).

### Revenue concentration and revenue quality

No public S-1 currently discloses Anthropic’s customer concentration, segment mix, deferred revenue, remaining performance obligations, revenue-recognition policy, churn, or related-party revenue balances ([Anthropic, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec)).

Anthropic disclosed on Apr. 20, 2026 that more than 1,000 customers each spend more than $1M annually, which is a positive breadth signal but not a substitute for a concentration schedule or top-customer revenue table ([Anthropic/Amazon, Apr. 20, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute)).

CoreWeave is a cautionary comp because its IPO prospectus disclosed Microsoft as 62% of 2024 revenue and its top two customers as 77% of 2024 revenue, showing that AI growth companies can have extreme customer concentration even at public scale ([CoreWeave SEC prospectus, Mar. 27, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000119312525067651/d899798d424b4.htm)).

### Related-party and circular-financing risk

Anthropic’s May 28, 2026 Series H announcement says the round included $15B of previously committed hyperscaler investments, including $5B from Amazon ([Anthropic Series H, May 28, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h)).

Anthropic’s Apr. 20, 2026 Amazon announcement says Amazon invested $5B that day, may invest up to an additional $20B, had previously invested $8B, and remains Anthropic’s primary training and cloud provider for mission-critical workloads ([Anthropic/Amazon, Apr. 20, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute)).

Anthropic’s Apr. 20, 2026 Amazon announcement also says Anthropic is committing more than $100B over ten years to AWS technologies and securing up to 5GW of new capacity to train and run Claude ([Anthropic/Amazon, Apr. 20, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute)).

Broadcom’s Apr. 6, 2026 Form 8-K says Broadcom, Google, and Anthropic expanded a strategic collaboration under which Anthropic will access about 3.5GW of next-generation TPU-based AI compute beginning in 2027, and the 8-K states that consumption depends on Anthropic’s continued commercial success ([Broadcom SEC 8-K, Apr. 6, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1730168/000119312526144028/d87999d8k.htm)).

The S-1 must therefore be checked for whether hyperscalers are investors, vendors, strategic partners, creditors, customers, or all of the above, because circular arrangements can distort revenue durability, margins, and capital needs ([Anthropic/Amazon, Apr. 20, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute); [Broadcom SEC 8-K, Apr. 6, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1730168/000119312526144028/d87999d8k.htm)).

### IPO access, day-1 risk, and lock-up risk

Investor.gov says underwriters and issuing companies control the IPO process, have wide latitude in allocating IPO shares, and that no brokerage firm can guarantee an individual investor will be able to purchase IPO shares ([Investor.gov IPO glossary, accessed Jun. 12, 2026](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/i)).

FINRA Rule 5131 requires book-running lead managers to report final allocations to institutional investors and aggregate sales to retail investors, which underscores that retail allocation is a process outcome rather than an entitlement ([FINRA Rule 5131, accessed Jun. 12, 2026](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/5131)).

Investor.gov states that lock-up agreements prohibit insiders, employees, friends and family, and large shareholders from selling shares for a set period after an IPO ([Investor.gov IPO glossary, accessed Jun. 12, 2026](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/i)).

Figma’s 2025 IPO shows why lock-up language matters: its prospectus had a lock-up ending on the earlier of the second trading day after second-quarter earnings following the latest included financial statements or 180 days after the prospectus date, and Figma later traded at a much lower public multiple in June 2026 than its IPO-era annualized revenue multiple ([Figma SEC prospectus, Jul. 30-31, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1579878/000162828025037014/figma424b4.htm); [Yahoo Finance FIG analysis/key statistics, accessed Jun. 12, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/FIG/analysis/)).

For a $300K-$500K deployment, buying day-1 in the open market creates price-discovery and float-squeeze risk, while waiting until after the S-1 and early lock-up dynamics are visible sacrifices upside optionality for materially better diligence visibility ([Investor.gov IPO glossary, accessed Jun. 12, 2026](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/i); [FINRA Rule 5131, accessed Jun. 12, 2026](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/5131)).

## Bottom line

Anthropic is real and exceptional on revenue growth, with verified run-rate revenue moving from about $9B at year-end 2025 to more than $47B in May 2026 ([Anthropic/Amazon, Apr. 20, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute); [Anthropic Series H, May 28, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h)).

The current private mark is also already aggressive at 20.5x verified run-rate revenue, and the absence of a public S-1 makes margin, burn, concentration, governance, related-party, and lock-up risks impossible to underwrite with public-company rigor ([Anthropic Series H, May 28, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h); [Anthropic, Jun. 1, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec)).

The recommended action is to cap entry at **14x-16x verified run-rate revenue** until the public S-1 is available, and to walk away from any allocation priced near **$1T** unless the filing confirms software-like margins, diversified revenue, benign governance, and limited post-lock-up supply risk ([Anthropic Series H, May 28, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h); [Harvard Law Review, Apr. 10, 2025](https://harvardlawreview.org/print/vol-138/amoral-drift-in-ai-corporate-governance/)).
