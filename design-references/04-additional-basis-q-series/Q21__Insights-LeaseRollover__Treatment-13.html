Claude's recommendation A. The Brixton's two-channel encoding (Q20=B status hue + intensity) is the surface's payload — A preserves it intact and accepts a small interaction cost (horizontal scroll). B preserves data but kills the cross-floorplan column-comparison story that's the matrix's reason for existing: each card becomes its own visual unit, so "B1 has the strongest renewing" is no longer preattentive across rows. Magnitude-within-status reads numerically only (chip values), not via intensity.

Honest read on audience: per /basis, primary users are desk-bound underwriters on ≥1600px monitors. Narrow-canvas is a secondary surface (tablet review during deal screening). A's "scroll right to see Total" cost is acceptable in that context; the headline rollover-distribution read still lands on first paint via the visible status columns. Sticky leftmost FP column is the cross-platform table pattern at narrow widths — anchors row identity during scroll.
