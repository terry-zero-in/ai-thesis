Q28

Lease expirations + projected turnover · dual-metric viz

Old Q28 framing (count vs percent vs hybrid threshold on a single turnover bar) presumed turnover was the primary metric. Wrong frame. Lease expirations is the precise observable signal from the rent roll (baseline ≈8.33%/mo if expirations were evenly distributed across 12 months). Projected turnover is a derived estimate — expirations × (1 − retention) + mid-lease attrition — useful for capex/renovation cadence but undefended at threshold-cutpoint precision pending a retention-rate-assumption owner. New question: how to viz both metrics on a shared time axis so the institutional read — lease concentration vs J-curve attrition — lands at first paint. Bands re-anchored around the 8.33% baseline: Low <5% · Watch 5–12% · Heavy >12% on expirations only; turnover line ships unbanded.

Appears on: Studio → Turnover · canvas-default 1440px · 200-unit sample property · same dataset across A/B/C

Drill inheritance — defended-vs-derived metric pairing: Bars carry the severity rubric (lease expirations is precise, observable, defended via 8.33% baseline anchor per card-gold §4). Line carries the projection (turnover is derived from a retention-rate assumption with no defended owner yet — Q27 lock applies: any metric without rubric + audit + gaming-defense + traceability ships unbanded). The relationship between the two — line below bar = renewals working, line above bar = J-curve / mid-lease attrition — is the institutional read.

Same 12-month dataset across A/B/C (200u · peak Sep '27 at 18 expirations / 9.0% · est. turnover 13 / 6.5% · 50% retention assumed · J-curve Jan-Mar mid-lease attrition 7 / 5 / 3). Decision is on viz layout, not data.

Claude's recommendation A · Option F. Single % axis with bars (expirations, severity-banded) and overlay line (turnover, neutral) is the only viz where the relationship between the two metrics is the read. Three reasons it pulls ahead: (1) shared % axis avoids the dual-axis pitfall — both metrics are %s, so a second axis is unjustified; (2) line-bar relationship surfaces J-curve at first paint — January's line punching +2.25pp above its bar (line 4.75% > bar 2.5%) is the exact signal a buyer needs to spot pre-acquisition cleanup attrition; (3) one chart preserves the unity of the rollover/turnover question — they're not two questions, they're one question with two timelines (when leases end vs when units actually leave).

Honest read on B and C: B (dual-row) gives the cleanest separation and the easiest scan, but the J-curve pattern requires vertical eye-jumping to spot — implicit, not explicit. C (grouped bars) is parallel comparison at the cost of crowding the chart and dropping the line-vs-bar relationship entirely. A is the institutional answer; B is the conservative fallback if dual-metric overlay confuses a specific reader; C is rejected — 24 bars across 12 months violates Linear's density-without-clutter principle.
