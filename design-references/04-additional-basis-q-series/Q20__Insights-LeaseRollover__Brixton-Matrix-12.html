Brixton matrix — color ramp + cell treatment

The Brixton matrix shows lease-rollover composition: floorplan rows × lease-status columns (Renewed · MTM · NTV · Unknown · Total). Vacant units are excluded — vacancies aren't lease-rollover events. Question: how should cells encode information beyond the raw count, and what is the cell-level visual primitive?

Appears on: Insights → Lease Rollover · 6 floorplans × 4 status columns + Total = 30 cells · ~180 unit example

Drill inheritance — nuance vs. Q17 surface-mapping: Q17 lists Lease Rollover at row count 12-18 → ▼ chevron when ≤15. That's the matrix-row count. Per-cell drill is into the unit list inside that cell (0–200 units depending on floorplan × status), which sits in the >15 regime almost universally. Cell-level affordance therefore = arrow → slide-out, not chevron — consistent with Rent Upside and Collections Quality cells (drill-policy.md §1, §4). Different scope, different affordance — both rules hold without conflict.

Cell intensity = count low → high
Single-hue accent ramp (`#4D5BFF`). Cell intensity scales to count magnitude. Linear / Vercel style — clean, restrained, reads volume. Cost: a 28-unit cell and a 22-unit cell look nearly identical at panel scale; intra-status comparison loses precision, and the matrix's distribution story (which status owns which floorplan) is masked by the single-hue uniformity.

Each column owns a hue family from the proposed status tokens. Per-column intensity = magnitude within that status. Reads distribution by status preattentively — "C1 is mostly Unknown," "B2 is the NTV/MTM hotspot," "B1 has the strongest renewing." Property baseline · Renewed 60% · MTM 5% · NTV 10% · Unknown 25% −20pp · baseline · +23pp
Self-referential anomaly detector. Each cell colored by how much that floorplan's status share diverges from the property's own overall mix — cool blue = under-indexed, warm orange = over-indexed, near-baseline = card-bg neutral. C1 lights up: massively under-renewed, massively over-Unknown — the anomaly. B2 surfaces as a turnover cluster. Computable from the rent roll alone (no comp data). Reads "which floorplans behave differently from the building."
Claude's recommendation B. The matrix's headline question is "how is rollover distributed by status across floorplans?" — categorical-by-status. B answers that preattentively: hue carries the status, intensity carries the magnitude, two channels in one cell. A reads volume only and loses the distribution story. C is genuinely valuable as an anomaly detector (C1 lights up beautifully) but answers a secondary question ("which floorplans diverge from baseline?") — best as a v1.1 toggle on top of B, not in place of it. Status token canonical hex is a separate decision — locking B locks the encoding choice and the four token names; canonical hex follows in a post-lock spec session.
